Companion to the Wall Chart · Lectures 9–15

What changes,
what stays normal, and why

Twenty-five diseases as findings rather than narrative. Each names the protein and the pathway in one line, then states what actually moves, with the reason underneath it, before stating what holds steady and why. Only the findings that carry weight or need explaining are listed.

25 diseases 296 findings Changed first, unchanged second No comparisons — each disease stands alone

How to read it

Every finding is a heading that states the direction in words, with the mechanism in a sentence or two beneath it. Nothing is encoded in a symbol, so a heading can be read on its own and still say something complete.

A few findings in each disease are set off in colour and tagged. Those are the ones that pin the diagnosis down — the value a question turns on, or the one that would separate this from whatever else was on the list.

The findings that stay normal are not filler. In most of these diseases the negative is what carries the diagnosis, and the reason a value holds steady is usually a sharper statement of the mechanism than the abnormal value beside it.

I

Digestion

1
01

Lactose intolerance

Lactase — brush-border disaccharidase, carbohydrate digestion
What changes

A high stool osmotic gap

Unabsorbed lactose stays in the lumen as a small osmotically active solute and holds water there.

A low stool pH

Colonic bacteria ferment the lactose to short-chain fatty acids, which acidify the stool.

Pins it down

High breath hydrogen

Human cells make no H₂. Every ppm came from bacteria fermenting lactose that reached the colon — which is why the test is specific.

More stool, more flatus, more bloating

Osmotic water retention plus fermentation gas (H₂, CO₂, methane) distending the bowel.

Reducing substances present in the stool

Intact lactose is a reducing sugar and passes through undigested.

What stays normal

Blood glucose stays normal

Absorbing and handling glucose is entirely normal. The failure is upstream, at splitting the disaccharide.

Serum electrolytes and pH stay normal

The sugar never crosses the epithelium, so there is no systemic metabolic consequence.

The intestinal biopsy is normal in the primary form

Villous architecture is normal and only the enzyme is absent. Lactase sits at the villus tip, so it is the first brush-border activity lost to any mucosal injury and the last to recover, which is why secondary deficiency follows gastroenteritis or celiac disease and shows that underlying damage on biopsy.

Growth and nutrition stay normal

Only one sugar is affected and the calorie loss is small.

II

Glycolysis

2
02

Pyruvate kinase deficiency

Pyruvate kinase (PKLR, R isoform) — final step of glycolysis
What changes

Red cell ATP falls

The last ATP-generating step is lost, and the red cell has no mitochondria and no alternative source.

Pins it down

2,3-BPG rises

The block sits at the end, so 1,3-BPG dams and spills into the Rapoport–Luebering shunt.

The oxygen dissociation curve shifts right

2,3-BPG lowers hemoglobin's O₂ affinity, so unloading improves and symptoms are milder than the hemoglobin predicts.

Hemoglobin falls

ATP failure stops the Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase; cells dehydrate, stiffen and are destroyed in the spleen.

Reticulocytes rise

Marrow compensation. Rises further after splenectomy, because the released cells now survive in circulation.

Indirect bilirubin rises

Heme catabolism from extravascular hemolysis.

LDH rises

Released from lysed cells. This reflects hemolysis, not glycolytic flux.

Haptoglobin falls

Consumed binding whatever hemoglobin is freed into plasma. The fall is modest rather than dramatic, because the destruction is mainly extravascular, inside splenic macrophages.

Pyruvate and lactate inside the red cell fall

Their production is the step that is blocked.

Echinocytes appear on the smear

Membrane dehydration and rigidity. Prominent after splenectomy.

What stays normal

Blood glucose stays normal

Hepatic output runs on glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis, and gluconeogenesis bypasses this enzyme entirely at PEPCK.

Serum lactate stays normal

Red cells contribute almost nothing to the whole-body lactate pool, and every other tissue is unaffected.

Muscle and brain function stay normal

Those tissues express PKM, a separate gene, at full activity.

The Coombs test stays negative

The hemolysis is metabolic, not antibody-mediated.

Pins it down

Osmotic fragility stays normal

Membrane proteins are structurally normal and no spherocytes form.

Pins it down

A whole-blood enzyme assay reads falsely normal

Leukocytes and platelets express PKM2 at normal activity. White cells must be removed or the result reads normal.

03

PFK-1 deficiency (Tarui, GSD VII)

Phosphofructokinase-1, M subunit — committed step of glycolysis
What changes

Muscle PFK-1 activity falls to zero

Mature skeletal muscle builds the tetramer from M alone.

Red cell PFK-1 activity falls to about half

The red-cell enzyme is an M/L hybrid, so the L subunit partially compensates.

Lactate fails to rise with exercise

Lactate is a glycolytic product and glycolysis is what is blocked, so ischemic forearm testing produces no rise.

Ammonia rises with exercise

The purine nucleotide cycle runs in place of glycolysis.

CK rises

ATP-starved fibers cannot maintain their membranes and leak.

Myoglobin appears in serum and urine

Rhabdomyolysis on exertion.

Uric acid rises after exercise

ATP is degraded to AMP faster than it can be rebuilt, and surplus AMP is broken down to urate.

Pins it down

A glucose load makes exercise tolerance worse

Muscle cannot use the glucose, and the insulin it provokes suppresses the free fatty acids and ketones the fiber was actually running on. Feeding before exercise removes the only fuel still available.

Pins it down

2,3-BPG falls

The block sits far upstream of 1,3-BPG, so less substrate reaches the shunt. The curve left-shifts.

Pins it down

Reticulocytes and bilirubin rise

Half activity shortens red-cell survival and the marrow compensates.

Hemoglobin falls mildly

Compensated hemolysis rather than overt anemia.

What stays normal

Blood glucose stays normal

The liver builds its enzyme from the L subunit, and hepatic output depends on glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis regardless.

Cognition stays normal

Brain PFK-1 is built largely from the P and L subunits and retains substantial activity.

Muscle at rest and low intensity works normally

Oxidative phosphorylation on fatty acids covers the demand without needing fast glycolysis.

Glycogen structure stays normal

Branching and debranching enzymes are untouched.

Pyruvate to acetyl-CoA

2
04

PDH complex deficiency

PDH E1α (PDHA1, X-linked) — pyruvate into the TCA cycle
What changes

Blood lactate rises

Pyruvate cannot become acetyl-CoA, so it dams and exits through LDH.

Blood pyruvate rises

Nothing downstream is consuming it.

Alanine rises

ALT transaminates the surplus pyruvate. The reaction is near-equilibrium and has no gate, so alanine tracks pyruvate.

Tissue acetyl-CoA falls

Its only route from carbohydrate is the blocked step.

Brain ATP falls

The brain runs almost wholly on glucose oxidation, and every alternative fuel must still pass this gate.

The anion gap widens

Lactate is the unmeasured anion.

Structural brain lesions develop

Corpus callosum agenesis, cerebral atrophy, Leigh lesions — energy failure during a period of rapid development.

IV dextrose makes it worse

Glucose enters above the block, so the added carbon partitions straight into more lactate.

Pins it down

A ketogenic diet makes it better

Ketone bodies are metabolized directly to acetyl-CoA and enter beyond the closed door.

What stays normal
Pins it down

The lactate to pyruvate ratio does not rise

Both rise together. The ratio is set by cytosolic NADH/NAD⁺, which is untouched here, so it stays at or below its normal 10–20.

Pins it down

Blood glucose stays normal

Gluconeogenesis is entirely intact; pyruvate carboxylase and PEPCK are unaffected.

Ammonia stays normal

Oxaloacetate and therefore aspartate are available, so the urea cycle runs.

Fatty acid oxidation and ketone production stay normal

Nothing upstream of acetyl-CoA in the fat pathway is affected, so ketones remain available and may rise as an alternative fuel.

Supplemental oxygen changes nothing

The block is enzymatic, not a delivery problem, so supplemental O₂ changes nothing.

05

Arsenic poisoning

Lipoic acid bound by arsenite; GAPDH subverted by arsenate — pyruvate into the TCA cycle
What changes

Blood lactate and pyruvate rise

Arsenite locks both thiols of lipoic acid, disabling PDH E2 and α-KGDH, so pyruvate dams and the cycle stalls one step in.

The net ATP yield of glycolysis falls from two to zero

Arsenate substitutes for inorganic phosphate at GAPDH. The arsenate ester hydrolyzes spontaneously, so the phosphoglycerate kinase ATP is never collected.

Oxidative ATP production falls

Three lipoate-dependent complexes are disabled simultaneously.

Branched-chain ketoacids rise

BCKDH carries lipoate too and is taken out with the others.

The anion gap widens

Lactate.

The QT interval lengthens

Direct effect on repolarization; torsades is the acute cardiac risk.

Pins it down

Garlic breath and rice-water stools appear

Volatile arsenicals in expired air; secretory and mucosal injury in the gut.

Pins it down

Mees' lines, hyperkeratosis and hyperpigmentation appear

Arsenic binds keratin sulfhydryls; nail and skin changes appear weeks to months after exposure.

Pins it down

Urine arsenic is high on a 24-hour collection

The confirmatory test. Hair and nails record older exposure.

What stays normal

Blood glucose stays normal

Gluconeogenesis is largely intact — no bypass enzyme depends on lipoate.

Supplemental oxygen changes nothing

The lesion is enzyme poisoning, not hypoxia.

IV

Gluconeogenesis

2
06

Pyruvate carboxylase deficiency

Pyruvate carboxylase (biotin-dependent) — first step of gluconeogenesis and main anaplerotic refill
What changes

Blood glucose falls during fasting

With no oxaloacetate, gluconeogenesis cannot start.

Blood lactate rises

Pyruvate dams. Acetyl-CoA and NADH then accumulate, activate PDH kinase and shut PDH off as well, so the aerobic exit closes too.

Pins it down

The lactate to pyruvate ratio rises above 25

Loss of oxaloacetate breaks the malate–aspartate shuttle, so cytosolic NADH accumulates and drives LDH.

Alanine rises

ALT transaminates surplus pyruvate. Alanine tracks pyruvate; there is no separate alanine ratio.

Mitochondrial NADH falls

The cycle is not turning to generate it, so the matrix is relatively oxidized while the cytosol is reduced.

Pins it down

The beta-hydroxybutyrate to acetoacetate ratio falls

Reports the oxidized matrix. Paired with the high lactate:pyruvate, it is the fingerprint of NADH stranded on the wrong side of the membrane.

Pins it down

Ammonia rises

Aspartate donates the second urea nitrogen and comes from oxaloacetate. No oxaloacetate, no aspartate, stalled urea cycle.

Citrulline rises

Citrulline backs up behind argininosuccinate synthetase, the step that needs aspartate. Lysine and proline rise alongside it, while glutamine falls as it is consumed carrying nitrogen.

Ketones rise

Acetyl-CoA cannot enter the cycle without a carrier, so it is diverted to ketone bodies.

Brain ATP falls

The cycle cannot accept acetyl-CoA, and neurons depend most on oxidative ATP.

The anion gap widens

Lactate plus ketones.

IV dextrose corrects the glucose but not the acidosis

Dextrose removes the demand on the missing enzyme but adds carbon behind a closed door, so lactate and alanine persist or rise.

What stays normal

Liver size and glycogen stores stay normal

Glycogen synthesis and breakdown are untouched.

Hemoglobin and red cell survival stay normal

The red cell performs no gluconeogenesis and has no TCA cycle to refill.

Fatty acid oxidation stays normal

Intact — which is why ketones are available and why acetyl-CoA is abundant.

07

Von Gierke, GSD Ia and Ib

Glucose 6-phosphatase (Ib: G6P translocase) — shared final step of gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis
What changes

Blood glucose falls severely within three to four hours of a feed

Both routes to free glucose pass through this one enzyme, so losing it closes them together.

Pins it down

Blood lactate rises

Accumulated G6P is forced down glycolysis to pyruvate and then lactate. It also serves as an alternative brain fuel, which is why these children tolerate low glucose.

Pins it down

Ketones stay inappropriately low for the degree of hypoglycemia

Lactate substitutes as fuel and insulin is not fully suppressed, so the hypoglycemia is characteristically non-ketotic.

Pins it down

Uric acid rises

G6P is pushed into the pentose phosphate pathway to ribose 5-P and PRPP, driving purine turnover. Lactate additionally competes with urate at the renal transporter.

Triglycerides and cholesterol rise

Surplus G6P runs to acetyl-CoA, leaves as citrate and feeds lipogenesis. Eruptive xanthomas follow.

The liver becomes massively enlarged

Glycogen and fat accumulate together. Doll-like face and protuberant abdomen.

The kidneys enlarge

The enzyme is expressed there too, so the same accumulation occurs.

Growth slows

Chronic energy deficit through childhood.

Glucagon produces no rise in glucose

Glucagon mobilizes glycogen normally, but the exit step that releases free glucose is missing.

Neutrophils fall in type Ib only

The translocase is also required in neutrophils. Recurrent infection and inflammatory bowel disease follow.

What stays normal

Glycogen structure stays normal

Branching is normal. The glycogen is merely abundant, not malformed.

Spleen size stays normal

Deposition is hepatic and renal, following where the enzyme is expressed.

CK and muscle function stay normal

Muscle glycogenolysis feeds its own glycolysis and never needed this enzyme.

Glycogen

08

GSD 0

Glycogen synthase — glycogen synthesis
What changes
Pins it down

Blood glucose rises after a meal

There is no reservoir to store it in, so a meal's glucose has nowhere to go.

Lactate rises after a meal

The unstorable surplus overflows into glycolysis and runs to lactate.

Blood glucose falls during fasting

Nothing was stored, so a few hours later there is nothing to draw on.

Ketones rise during fasting

Fatty acid oxidation is intact, so the hypoglycemia is ketotic.

Pins it down

Liver glycogen content falls markedly

The defect itself — this is a deficiency of glycogen, not a storage of it.

Exertional syncope and arrhythmia occur in the muscle form

Cardiac and skeletal muscle cannot store glycogen, giving cardiomyopathy and sudden death risk on exertion.

What stays normal
Pins it down

Liver size stays normal

Nothing accumulates, so there is nothing to enlarge it.

Fasting lactate stays normal

Gluconeogenesis is intact and G6P is not accumulating.

Uric acid and triglycerides stay normal

No G6P backlog to divert into PRPP or lipogenesis.

09

Andersen, GSD IV

Branching enzyme (glucosyl 4:6 transferase) — glycogen synthesis
What changes
Pins it down

Glycogen is built long and unbranched

No α-1,6 branches are created, so chains grow straight and resemble plant amylopectin.

Glycogen solubility falls

Branching is what keeps glycogen hydrated. An unbranched glucan precipitates.

Transaminases rise

The insoluble deposit behaves as a foreign body inside hepatocytes and provokes inflammation.

Pins it down

The liver enlarges, then becomes cirrhotic

Inflammation progresses to fibrosis and cirrhosis, typically fatal by around five years without transplant.

Spleen size and portal pressure rise

Consequences of established cirrhosis.

INR rises

Loss of hepatic synthetic function; albumin falls in parallel.

What stays normal
Pins it down

Blood glucose stays largely normal

The small amount of glycogen present is still mobilizable and gluconeogenesis is untouched. The disease is structural, not energetic.

Lactate, uric acid and triglycerides stay normal

There is no G6P accumulation to divert into those routes.

CK stays normal in the classic hepatic form

Muscle is usually spared in the form that presents in infancy.

10

Cori (Forbes), GSD III

Debranching enzyme (4:4 transferase + α-1,6-glucosidase) — glycogenolysis
What changes

Blood glucose falls mildly

Phosphorylase strips a chain only to within four residues of a branch and then halts, so release stops there.

Pins it down

Ketones rise

Fatty acid oxidation is intact, so the hypoglycemia is ketotic.

Pins it down

Glycogen accumulates with short outer chains

A limit dextrin accumulates because branch points cannot be cleared.

The liver enlarges

Limit dextrin accumulation.

Transaminases rise

Hepatocyte distension. Usually improves after puberty.

CK rises in type IIIa

The same enzyme serves muscle, so myopathy and cardiomyopathy appear in the subtype that involves it.

Growth slows during childhood

Recovers with age as hepatic demand falls relative to body size.

A high-protein diet works

Gluconeogenic machinery is fully available to convert the amino acids into glucose.

What stays normal
Pins it down

Blood lactate stays normal

Gluconeogenesis is intact and no G6P backlog forms.

Uric acid stays normal

No PRPP overproduction, since G6P is not accumulating.

Triglycerides stay normal or rise only mildly

No large overflow of G6P into lipogenesis.

11

McArdle, GSD V

Myophosphorylase (PYGM) — muscle glycogenolysis
What changes

Lactate fails to rise with exercise

Muscle cannot mobilize its own glycogen, so no substrate reaches glycolysis during ischemic forearm testing.

Ammonia rises with exercise

The purine nucleotide cycle runs in place of glycolysis.

CK is high even at rest

Ongoing membrane instability in fibers that cannot meet their own ATP demand.

Myoglobin appears in the urine

Rhabdomyolysis on exertion, with risk of acute kidney injury from cast nephropathy.

Muscle glycogen content rises

It is deposited normally and simply cannot be retrieved.

Pins it down

A second wind arrives after about ten minutes

Fatty acid delivery and hepatic glucose output ramp up and take over the work glycogen should have done.

What stays normal

Blood glucose stays normal

Liver phosphorylase is PYGL, a different gene, and is fully functional.

Liver size stays normal

Hepatic glycogen turnover is normal.

Cognition stays normal

The brain does not depend on muscle glycogen.

Pins it down

Hemoglobin and reticulocytes stay normal

The red cell holds no glycogen and does not use this isoform.

Glycogen structure stays normal

Branching enzymes are untouched; only the quantity rises.

12

Hers, GSD VI (and GSD IX)

Liver phosphorylase (PYGL); GSD IX, phosphorylase kinase — hepatic glycogenolysis
What changes

Blood glucose falls mildly

The liver cannot break down its own glycogen, but the deficiency is characteristically partial.

Pins it down

Ketones rise

Fatty acid oxidation is intact, so the hypoglycemia is ketotic.

The liver enlarges, often markedly

Glycogen accumulates because it cannot be mobilized.

Liver glycogen content rises

Structurally normal glycogen, simply not retrievable.

Transaminases rise

Hepatocyte distension. Improves with age.

Growth slows mildly

Chronic mild limitation of hepatic glucose output.

What stays normal
Pins it down

Blood lactate stays normal

Gluconeogenesis is fully intact and defends the blood sugar.

Uric acid and triglycerides stay normal

No G6P backlog to divert into PRPP or lipogenesis.

Pins it down

CK and muscle function stay normal

Muscle uses PYGM, a separate gene.

Glycogen structure stays normal

Branching and debranching are normal.

13

Pompe, GSD II

Acid α-1,4-glucosidase (acid maltase) — lysosomal glycogen degradation
What changes

Glycogen accumulates inside lysosomes

Glycogen delivered to lysosomes by autophagy cannot be digested, so it accumulates in the compartment that cannot clear it.

Pins it down

The heart becomes massively enlarged

Cardiac muscle turns over the most glycogen; swelling lysosomes distort and destroy the contractile apparatus.

Muscle tone falls

Lysosomal rupture destroys fibers, giving the floppy infant.

CK rises

Ongoing muscle destruction.

The tongue enlarges

Macroglossia from glycogen accumulation in the same muscle type.

Diaphragm strength falls

Disproportionately early involvement; the presenting feature in late-onset forms.

Pins it down

The ECG shows a short PR and high-voltage QRS

Glycogen-laden myocardium alters conduction and voltage. Characteristic of the infantile form.

What stays normal
Pins it down

Blood glucose stays normal

Cytoplasmic glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis are entirely untouched.

Glycogen structure stays normal

Normal branching. Only its location is wrong.

Blood lactate stays normal

No cytosolic pathway is blocked.

Uric acid and triglycerides stay normal

No G6P accumulation anywhere.

14

Alcoholic hypoglycemia

No enzyme lost — ADH and ALDH raise the cytosolic NADH:NAD⁺ ratio
What changes

The cytosolic NADH to NAD+ ratio rises

Each of the two oxidation steps reduces an NAD⁺, so two NADH are generated per molecule of ethanol.

Blood glucose falls, but only during fasting

Pyruvate, oxaloacetate and DHAP are all pushed to their reduced products, stripping all three gluconeogenic entry substrates at once.

Lactate rises

LDH is near equilibrium and is driven toward lactate by mass action.

Uric acid rises

Lactate competes with urate at the renal excretion transporter.

Triglycerides and hepatic fat rise

DHAP is diverted to glycerol 3-phosphate, supplying the triglyceride backbone.

Beta-hydroxybutyrate rises

The raised NADH pushes acetoacetate toward β-hydroxybutyrate.

Pins it down

The urine ketone test reads falsely low

The test detects acetoacetate, not β-hydroxybutyrate, so it reads low in a deeply ketotic patient.

Pins it down

The AST to ALT ratio rises above two to one

Alcohol depletes pyridoxal phosphate, and ALT depends on it more heavily than AST.

The anion gap widens

Lactate plus ketoacids.

What stays normal
Pins it down

Blood glucose stays normal in a fed patient

Glycogenolysis requires no NAD⁺, so full glycogen stores fully protect. Fasting is a precondition.

Every enzyme activity stays normal

Nothing is missing or inhibited. Only the redox balance has moved.

Everything returns to normal once the ethanol clears

The shift is fully reversible because no protein was damaged.

15

Methanol poisoning

No enzyme lost — ADH and ALDH convert methanol to formate
What changes

Formate accumulates

The actual toxin. Methanol itself is comparatively inert.

Serum pH falls

Severe metabolic acidosis from formate plus the lactate generated behind the blocked respiratory chain.

The anion gap widens

Formate is the unmeasured anion.

Lactate rises

Formate inhibits cytochrome c oxidase, forcing an anaerobic shift.

Pins it down

Visual acuity falls

Formate concentrates in the optic nerve. Patients describe a snowstorm; the disc may be hyperemic.

Pins it down

The osmolar gap comes first, the anion gap second

The parent alcohol is osmotically active; as it is converted, the osmolar gap closes and the anion gap opens.

Pins it down

Nothing happens for the first twelve to twenty-four hours

Toxicity requires the metabolite to accumulate, so the patient looks well at first.

Cytosolic NADH rises

Each of the two oxidation steps reduces an NAD⁺.

What stays normal

Blood glucose stays normal

The gluconeogenic enzymes themselves are intact.

Everything looks normal in the first hours

Nothing toxic has been made yet, which is what makes early presentation deceptive.

Methanol is cleared unchanged once fomepizole is given

Blocking ADH means methanol is excreted unchanged rather than converted, so the level falls slowly and harmlessly.

VII

The sugars that are not glucose

5
16

Essential fructosuria

Fructokinase — fructose entry
What changes

Blood fructose rises

Never phosphorylated, so never trapped inside a cell.

Urine fructose rises

Freely filtered and incompletely reabsorbed.

Reducing substances appear in the urine

Fructose is a reducing sugar. This is usually how the condition is found.

What stays normal
Pins it down

The urine glucose dipstick stays negative

The dipstick is specific for glucose, so a positive reducing test with a negative dipstick indicates the sugar is not glucose.

Blood glucose stays normal

Glucose handling is untouched.

ATP and inorganic phosphate stay normal

No phosphorylated intermediate forms, so no phosphate is sequestered.

Uric acid stays normal

ATP is never depleted, so no AMP is degraded to urate.

Pins it down

Liver and kidney function stay normal

Nothing is trapped inside cells to injure them.

Growth and development stay normal

The condition is benign and needs no treatment.

17

Hereditary fructose intolerance

Aldolase B — fructose entry
What changes

Fructose 1-phosphate accumulates inside cells

Fructokinase upstream is fast and unregulated, so F1P is made at speed and cannot be cleared.

Intracellular inorganic phosphate falls

Every molecule of F1P formed locks up one Pi.

ATP falls

Cannot be regenerated without free phosphate.

Uric acid rises

AMP accumulates as ATP falls and is degraded to urate.

Pins it down

Blood glucose falls about thirty minutes after fructose

Low ATP and trapped phosphate inhibit both glycogen phosphorylase and gluconeogenesis, closing both hepatic routes to glucose.

Glucagon produces no rise in glucose

Glycogenolysis is inhibited, so the hormone has nothing to act on.

Lactate rises

Energy failure and pyruvate accumulation.

Transaminases and bilirubin rise

Hepatocyte injury from phosphate sequestration.

INR rises

Loss of hepatic synthetic function; albumin falls in parallel.

Proximal renal tubular dysfunction develops

Aminoaciduria, phosphaturia and renal tubular acidosis, from the same phosphate trapping in tubular cells.

Reducing substances appear in the urine

Fructose spills into the urine.

What stays normal

Everything stays normal while fructose is avoided

The substrate must be present for anything to happen. Removing fructose, sucrose and sorbitol resolves the disease.

Pins it down

Blood glucose stays normal on breast milk

Lactose contains no fructose, so infants are well until weaning.

Pins it down

Dental caries are notably absent

Survivors develop a strong aversion to sweet foods.

18

Galactokinase deficiency

Galactokinase — Leloir pathway
What changes

Blood and urine galactose rise

It cannot be phosphorylated, so it accumulates free and is filtered.

Reducing substances appear in the urine

Galactose is a reducing sugar.

Galactitol accumulates in the lens

Aldose reductase reduces the excess galactose, and no dehydrogenase can take galactitol any further.

Cataracts develop in infancy

Trapped galactitol is osmotically active, draws water into lens fibers and aggregates their proteins. Presents as failure to fix and follow, or an absent red reflex.

Lens NADPH falls modestly

Spent by aldose reductase, adding a small oxidative component.

What stays normal
Pins it down

Galactose 1-phosphate is never formed

None is formed at all, and that single absence is why no organ is injured.

Pins it down

Liver function stays normal

Nothing is trapped intracellularly to sequester phosphate.

Kidney function stays normal

Same reason.

Blood glucose stays normal

Glucose handling is untouched.

Pins it down

Cognitive development stays normal

No CNS injury occurs. Growth and development are normal.

The urine glucose dipstick stays negative

Specific for glucose.

19

Classical galactosemia

GALT (galactose 1-phosphate uridyltransferase) — Leloir pathway
What changes
Pins it down

Galactose 1-phosphate accumulates in red cells

Galactokinase upstream still works at full rate, so the intermediate is made and then dammed. This is both the cause of the disease and the marker followed on treatment.

Blood glucose falls

Gal-1-P inhibits phosphoglucomutase, which glycogenolysis needs, on top of the liver injury itself.

Transaminases and bilirubin rise

Hepatocyte injury from phosphate and UDP sequestration. Progresses to cirrhosis untreated.

INR rises

Loss of hepatic synthetic function.

Galactitol accumulates in the lens

Overflow galactose is reduced by aldose reductase, producing cataracts.

Proximal renal tubular dysfunction develops

Aminoaciduria and renal tubular acidosis from injury to tubular cells by the same trapped ester.

Pins it down

The risk of neonatal E. coli sepsis rises

Gal-1-P impairs neutrophil function. Characteristic enough to prompt testing on its own.

Red cell GALT activity is low

The confirmatory assay, after newborn screening flags it.

Reducing substances appear in the urine

Galactose spills into the urine.

Pins it down

Ovarian insufficiency and learning difficulty occur despite treatment

The body synthesizes its own Gal-1-P from UDP-glucose regardless of what is eaten, so these are not fully preventable.

What stays normal

The urine glucose dipstick stays negative

Specific for glucose, so it misses the galactose entirely.

Fatty acid oxidation and ketones stay normal

Untouched by the defect.

Liver, kidney and lens recover on a galactose-free diet

Removing the substrate stops Gal-1-P forming, and those injuries resolve.

20

Diabetic cataract, retinopathy and neuropathy

No enzyme lost — aldose reductase unopposed where sorbitol dehydrogenase is absent (polyol pathway)
What changes

Sorbitol accumulates inside the affected cells

In lens, retina, Schwann cells and kidney. Aldose reductase has a high Kₘ and no allosteric brake, so flux rises in proportion to hyperglycemia, and those tissues cannot oxidize the product onward.

Water is drawn osmotically into those cells

Sorbitol is polar and cannot cross the membrane, so it is trapped and holds water.

Tissue NADPH falls

Consumed by aldose reductase at the first step.

Reduced glutathione falls

Glutathione reductase needs the NADPH the polyol pathway has just spent.

Oxidative injury rises

Antioxidant defense weakens at the same moment the osmotic load rises.

Nerve conduction velocity falls

Schwann cell swelling and secondary demyelination.

Cataracts develop

Lens fiber swelling with protein aggregation.

HbA1c rises

Non-enzymatic glycation runs alongside and records the same glucose exposure.

What stays normal
Pins it down

Enzyme quantity and activity stay normal

Nothing is deficient. Only the substrate is elevated, which is why glycemic control reverses the flux.

Pins it down

Sorbitol stays normal in seminal vesicle, liver and ovary

Those tissues have sorbitol dehydrogenase and clear it onward to fructose.

ATP stays normal

The pathway spends no ATP at either step.

NADPH

5
21

G6PD deficiency

Glucose 6-phosphate dehydrogenase (X-linked) — oxidative pentose phosphate pathway
What changes

Red cell NADPH falls

This pathway is the red cell's only source, and with no nucleus it cannot replace the enzyme as the protein degrades.

Reduced glutathione falls

Glutathione reductase has no NADPH with which to regenerate it.

Hydrogen peroxide and hydroxyl radical accumulate

Peroxide goes uncleared, and heme iron drives it on to the hydroxyl radical by Fenton chemistry.

Heinz bodies form

Oxidized hemoglobin cysteines cross-link and the protein precipitates onto the inner membrane.

Pins it down

Bite cells appear on the smear

Splenic macrophages bite the Heinz bodies out of passing cells.

Hemoglobin falls in episodes

Combined intravascular lysis and splenic destruction, roughly a day after the trigger.

Reticulocytes rise

Marrow response over the following week.

Indirect bilirubin and LDH rise

Heme catabolism and cell lysis.

Haptoglobin falls

Consumed binding free hemoglobin.

The urine turns dark brown

Hemoglobin freed intravascularly is filtered.

The episode ends itself within about a week

The oldest and most deficient cells lyse first and are replaced by enzyme-rich young cells.

What stays normal

Blood glucose stays normal

The pathway makes no ATP and consumes very little glucose.

Pins it down

The enzyme assay reads falsely normal during the crisis

The deficient cells have already lysed and reticulocytes have high activity. Wait several weeks before testing.

The Coombs test stays negative

The hemolysis is oxidative, not immune.

Pins it down

The blood count stays normal between episodes

In the common variants there is no baseline hemolysis without a trigger.

22

Thiamine deficiency

Thiamine pyrophosphate — cofactor for PDH E1, α-KGDH, BCKDH and transketolase
What changes

Blood lactate and pyruvate rise

E1 cannot decarboxylate pyruvate, so it dams and exits through LDH.

ATP falls in brain and heart

Two consecutive oxidative steps are lost, and these tissues have the highest and least interruptible oxidative demand.

Pins it down

Red cell transketolase activity is low

The diagnostic assay, measured before and after adding TPP. A large activation on adding TPP confirms the deficiency.

Branched-chain ketoacids rise

BCKDH is TPP-dependent as well.

Flux through the cycle stalls at alpha-ketoglutarate

The cycle stalls one step in, compounding the block at its entrance.

Dry beriberi develops

Symmetric peripheral neuropathy from axonal energy failure.

Wet beriberi develops

High-output cardiac failure with edema, from myocardial energy failure and peripheral vasodilation.

Wernicke encephalopathy develops

Confusion, ophthalmoplegia and ataxia from energy failure in mammillary bodies and periaqueductal gray.

Korsakoff syndrome develops

Confabulation and irreversible anterograde amnesia once mammillary damage is established.

Pins it down

A glucose load makes it acutely worse

Glucose consumes the last remaining thiamine, which is why thiamine is given first.

What stays normal

Blood glucose stays normal

Gluconeogenesis is largely intact; no bypass enzyme is TPP-dependent.

The enzyme proteins themselves stay normal

The apoenzymes are present and functional. Only the cofactor is missing, so repletion restores activity.

23

Chronic granulomatous disease

NADPH oxidase (most often gp91phox, X-linked) — the respiratory burst
What changes

Superoxide production in the phagolysosome falls

The complex cannot assemble from its cytosolic and membrane subunits, so no electrons reach O₂.

Hydrogen peroxide falls

Superoxide dismutase has no substrate.

Hypochlorous acid falls

Myeloperoxidase has no peroxide to work with, so the killing cascade is dark end to end.

Intracellular killing fails

The organism is engulfed normally and then survives inside the phagocyte.

Pins it down

Infections with catalase-positive organisms rise

Catalase-negative organisms generate their own H₂O₂, which the crippled phagocyte can borrow; catalase-positive ones destroy it and leave the cell with nothing.

Granulomas form

Persisting live organisms drive continuous macrophage recruitment and epithelioid transformation.

Pins it down

The nitroblue tetrazolium test stays yellow

No superoxide is made to reduce the dye to blue formazan.

The dihydrorhodamine test shows no fluorescence

Same failure, read by flow cytometry. More sensitive and now the preferred test.

What stays normal

Blood glucose stays normal

NADPH is being spent, not the glucose supply failing.

The neutrophil count stays normal

Production, chemotaxis and margination are all normal.

Pins it down

Phagocytosis and phagolysosome fusion stay normal

Recognition, engulfment and fusion are intact. Only killing fails.

Immunoglobulins stay normal

B- and T-cell function are normal, though levels may be raised from chronic antigenic stimulation.

24

ALS (Lou Gehrig disease), SOD1 familial form

Cu-Zn superoxide dismutase 1 — misfolded, not absent (ROS clearance)
What changes

Misfolded SOD1 aggregates accumulate

Point substitutions destabilize the fold. The aggregates sequester chaperones, disrupt mitochondria and impair axonal transport.

Motor neuron survival falls

Motor neurons have the longest axons and highest metabolic load in the nervous system and tolerate that burden least.

Upper motor neuron signs appear

Spasticity and hyperreflexia, from corticospinal degeneration.

Lower motor neuron signs appear

Atrophy and fasciculations, in the same limb as the upper signs — the combination is the diagnostic pattern.

EMG shows denervation potentials

Fibrillations and fasciculations across multiple root and nerve territories.

Respiratory muscle strength falls

Progressive diaphragm and intercostal denervation; the eventual cause of death.

What stays normal
Pins it down

Dismutase activity itself stays normal

Largely intact. The harm is a toxic gain of function, not loss of the reaction, which is exactly why an enzyme assay cannot diagnose this.

Pins it down

Sensation stays normal

Sensory neurons are not affected.

Pins it down

Eye movements and bladder control stay normal

Characteristically preserved until very late.

Cognition usually stays normal

Preserved in most patients, though a frontotemporal dementia overlap exists in a minority.

25

Rhabdomyolysis on a statin

No enzyme lost — CYP3A4 inhibited (cytochrome P450)
What changes

The statin's plasma concentration rises

Grapefruit furanocoumarins, macrolides, azoles, protease inhibitors or certain calcium channel blockers block the clearance route.

CK rises, often above ten thousand

Depleted mevalonate-pathway products destabilize the myocyte membrane and impair its mitochondria, and fibers lyse.

Myoglobin appears in serum and urine

Released from lysed fibers and freely filtered.

Potassium and phosphate rise

Both are principally intracellular and are released in bulk from damaged muscle.

Creatinine rises

Filtered myoglobin precipitates as casts and is directly nephrotoxic to tubular cells.

Calcium falls

Deposits in damaged muscle. May rebound to hypercalcemia during recovery as it is mobilized.

Uric acid rises

Purine released from lysed muscle and degraded.

The anion gap widens

Organic acids released from muscle plus developing renal failure.

Pins it down

The urine dipstick is positive for blood with no red cells on microscopy

The dipstick detects heme, and myoglobin carries one. The discrepancy is the tell.

What stays normal

The CYP3A4 gene and protein stay normal

The enzyme is inhibited, not absent, so clearance returns once the interacting drug is withdrawn.

Blood glucose stays normal

No carbohydrate pathway is involved.

Pins it down

Muscle was normal before the interaction

The patient tolerated the statin alone. Onset follows a new prescription or a change in diet.
